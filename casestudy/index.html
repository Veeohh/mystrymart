A1 The market

Collectors want the person behind the work.

The top of the art market is shrinking while the entry level grows, and new collectors increasingly find and buy from artists directly. The opportunity was a door into collecting that starts with a living artist instead of a price tag.

−45%

Trophy art is cooling

In 2024, global art sales fell 12% to $57.5B, and auction sales of works over $10M fell 45%. Meanwhile, works under $5,000 grew 7% in value and 13% in number sold.

Art Basel & UBS Art Market Report 2025 ↗
63%

Buying straight from artists

63% of collectors bought directly from artists in 2024–25, through studio visits, commissions or social media. That is more than double the rate two years earlier.

Affordable Art Fair, citing Art Basel & UBS ↗
68%

Discovery starts on a phone

Instagram is the main way collectors under 45 discover art, and 68% say a social post has directly sent them to see art in person.

A2 The brief

Two audiences.
One locked door.

Original art had a two-sided access problem. Buyers felt it wasn’t for them, and emerging artists had nowhere affordable to meet those buyers. The strategy had to open the door from both sides at once. Tap a card to see what we built.

Side 1 · The collector

“Art isn’t for people like me.”

  • Gallery prices put original art out of reach for most people.
  • Gallery labels list the medium, size and price, but say little about the person who made it.
  • Walking up to an artist’s table at a market or fair can feel awkward, with pressure to buy.
  • A first purchase feels like a big, serious decision.
↻ Tap to see what we built
What we built for collectors

Make the first piece easy.

  • A machine everyone already knows how to use: tap, pick a number, grab.
  • Real, original art for $10 to $100, with most bags around $20.
  • No sales pitch and no pressure. The art picks you.
  • A QR code on every bag introduces the artist: who they are, how they work and what you might get.
  • Fun for kids and adults, and a keepsake from a trip or a great date.
↻ Tap to flip back
Side 2 · The artist

“Nobody can find my work.”

  • Galleries are hard for emerging artists to get into.
  • Making social media content so people notice your work isn’t every artist’s strong suit.
  • Fairs and markets are expensive: a table, travel and supplies, and some days you leave with no sales.
  • Hard to turn one buyer into a lasting fan.
↻ Tap to see what we built
What we built for artists

A storefront with no cover charge.

  • No upfront costs, no table fees and no travel. Artists earn 50% of every sale.
  • A slot in machines at museums, venues and art fairs, selling while they work.
  • A page on our site for every artist, linked from the QR code on each bag.
  • An artist portal for requests, tracking and monthly earnings.

A3 The name

Drop the E.
Read the ending.

We knew the name had to say mystery, because the product is a blind bag. Our first choice, Mystery Market, was already taken on the federal trademark register. Shortening it to Mystery Mart was taken too. So we looked for a spelling that still reads as “mystery,” dropped the E, and found the mission hiding in the letters. Tap the pink E.

B1 The mission

Discover. Connect. Support.

Every decision, from the bag to the website, serves one of three jobs. For some artists, a Mystry Mart bag is their very first sale. For some collectors, it’s the first piece of art they’ve ever bought.

DISCOVER

The art picks you

  • Blind bags take the pressure out of choosing, so nobody needs to “know art” to start.
  • Artist first: kids and adults browse the artists before they choose a slot, so they learn along the way.
  • Building the next generation of collectors, one bag at a time.
CONNECT

Every bag leads to a person

  • Scan a bag to open the artist’s own page: their background, who they are, their work and how they make it.
  • See what you could get from that slot before you buy.
  • Collectors follow artists, find them at fairs and commission bigger work.
SUPPORT

Real art, real income

  • Artists earn 50% of every sale, with no upfront costs.
  • Own original art without the gallery price tag, and know a living artist got paid.
  • A keepsake from a trip, a museum visit or a great date.

What’s inside the bag

A piece of art, and the person who made it.

Artists pack their own bags, so every one is a little different. Most include the work plus a way to meet the artist.

  1. The artworkHere, a hand-numbered giclée print made only for our Mystry Mart collection.
  2. The artist’s storyA statement card on who they are and what their work explores.
  3. A note on the pieceHow it was made, plus a thank-you for supporting independent art.
  4. A way to follow themA business card with their website, Instagram and email.

A vending machine that ends on your phone.

The machine is the physical hook. The QR code on every bag carries the moment online, where a surprise turns into a follow, a share and a returning fan. Try it: click any bag to scan it and meet a random artist from our roster, then tap to buy.

B3 The model

50/50. No cover charge.

Artists consign their work and keep half of every sale, with no fees to get in. They set their own prices, so the business grows only when artists get paid. Drag the sliders to see an example month.

B4 The back office

From 15 hours of spreadsheets to 2 hours of clicks.

Every machine meant orders, tracking numbers, stock counts, payouts and a lot of emails, all kept in spreadsheets and inboxes. We built two linked portals with AI: an internal HQ for our team and a private portal for every artist. Each action on one side updates the other automatically.

B5 The machine as a stage

Every machine is a canvas.

When a new space welcomes us, we run a design competition and put out ads to find the artist. Artists pitch a design, the venue picks the one that fits, and the winner paints the machine, creating a bespoke experience for every location. Every placement starts with an artist getting a public commission.

C1 The placement

From a Time Out story to the Brooklyn Museum.

We turned photos and videos from the first machine into a press kit, and Time Out picked up the story. After reading it, the Brooklyn Museum’s leadership reached out. The machine went in as a 3-month pilot in October 2024 and became a long-term partnership.

D2 How I work

Strategy, operations and AI, in one seat.

Mystry Mart has two co-founders. Jacob Bell runs finance, machine maintenance and restocking. I lead strategy, brand, partnerships and the tools we run on. Here is what my side looks like in a strategy role.

CREATIVE STRATEGY

Make the idea do the selling

  • Naming: found a trademark-free name that states the mission.
  • Experience: a blind-bag format that lowers the barrier to buying original art.
  • Brand: logo, packaging, machine designs, social and website.
  • PR: press kits that earned CBS, Time Out, PIX11 and more.
OPERATIONS

Make it run without me

  • Partnerships: a zero-risk 3-month pilot that converts to permanent, plus Superblue, Meow Wolf and the New Museum signed for 2027.
  • Contracts: artist and venue agreements, wholesale and consignment.
  • Supply chain: overseas packaging manufacturers and inventory planning.
  • Growth: $1.5K to $400K+ in revenue in two years.
AI

Build the tools myself

  • Artist portal: cut weekly ops from 15+ hours to about 2.
  • Website: an interactive online machine, built in 3 days.
  • Campaigns: 667 applications in 5 days on a $93 ad budget.
  • Feedback loops: surveys and DMs turned into features, like QR codes.
